# Legibility

Why legibility matters and what it covers. Terms are defined in [taxonomy.md](taxonomy.md).

Legibility is generally important. **Illegibility kills an incremental game twice:**

1. **At the start.** Disorientation is a bad feeling. Disoriented players just leave.
2. **Later, by making promises bad promises.** If the player has no way to estimate when a promise will be fulfilled, they may leave. If they expect it soon, and after that time they're not even closer, they leave.

**Legibility is about being able to predict how long progress takes, so the player stays around for the next thing.**

---

## Two kinds of legibility

### Legibility of the game (UI and learning)

**The whole unfolding concept is a legibility concept.** The cognitive load of learning the game is gradual: the player always has just one new thing to learn, and by the time the next thing comes in, everything else is muscle memory.

### Legibility of mechanics and progression

The rules have to be legible enough for the player to make **calculations or estimations about how long things take**.

This is why curves are fixed by changing constants or unfolding, not by special-case rules (see [curves.md](curves.md#fixing-a-curve)).

---

## Every element earns its place

Each rule, resource, and item the player has to learn adds cognitive load. Each one should do a job no other element already does. More elements are not more depth.
