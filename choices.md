# Choices

Principles for player choices. Terms are defined in [taxonomy.md](taxonomy.md).

Choices matter, but **they should never be artificial.**

## Where choices come from

Choices are **downstream of unfolding**, and of mechanics having **allocation options and a bit of scarcity**. A good game makes you choose, and makes those choices meaningful.

## Measuring meaningfulness

**How much better is optimal play than "buy cheapest"?**

- If buying the cheapest thing is optimal, or very close to optimal, the game has no real choices.
- (Sometimes "buy cheapest" isn't even an option, e.g. when many things cost the same. The question still applies: how much does thinking beat not thinking?)

## Good choices

The happy path:

1. The player asks: *what's the next thing I want to get to? Which promise is most compelling to me?*
2. They make allocation choices that get them there faster.

A good choice:

- **Is reversible.**
- **Has no terrible option.**
- **Gives agency** to decide which direction to progress in, and to progress further in that direction.

Some players will choose to progress evenly in all directions. **That's viable too.**

## Bad choices

An **irreversible either/or made without information.** "You can only choose one of these two," the decision is permanent, and the player has nothing to base a good choice on.
