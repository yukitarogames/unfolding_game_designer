# Fun

What makes a mechanic worth playing. Terms are defined in [taxonomy.md](taxonomy.md).

Promises, curves, legibility, and pacing keep a player going. None of them makes the game fun. A perfectly paced game with a boring verb is a boring game.

**Fun comes first. Structure serves it.**

---

## Each mechanic is a good game on its own

A [mechanic](taxonomy.md#mechanic) can be a standalone game, so hold it to that: if this mechanic were the whole game, would someone want to play it?

A mechanic that only makes sense as a booster for another mechanic is a feature, not a mechanic.

## The verb has decisions in it

The player's core action should involve a real decision, not just "can I afford it? click."

- **Decisions come from things interacting.** What the player buys or places changes the value of what's already there, and of what comes next.
- **Measure it.** The test from [choices.md](choices.md#measuring-meaningfulness) applies to the verb: how much does thinking beat buying the cheapest thing? If the answer is "barely", the verb is boring, however good the pacing.

## Upgrades change how you play

A number going up is the floor, not the goal.

- **The best upgrades change the rules:** a new option, a new shape, a new interaction, a new way to combine what the player already has.
- **After a good upgrade, the player's best move is different.** An upgrade that leaves the player doing exactly the same thing, only faster, is filler.
- **Filler is fine between the good ones.** A mechanic made only of filler is boring.

> **Analogy:** a faster car gets you down the same roads sooner. A boat takes you places you couldn't go.

## Options arrive when they're affordable

Within a mechanic, **price is the gate.** When the player can afford something, they can buy it.

- Don't lock items, space, or options behind flags, tiers, or milestones when a price would do the job. An arbitrary lock feels like the game holding the player back.
- A price is also a promise: the player can see it and estimate how far away it is.

## Interactions are depth, not clutter

- **A mechanic whose items don't affect each other is a shopping list.** Rules that refer to each other are where depth lives.
- **An interaction between things the player already knows is the cheapest depth there is.** There's nothing new to learn, and a lot more to think about.
- **Make interactions visible.** The player should see them happen, in the space or on the item, so they learn them by playing.

## Bold over safe

- **A design that breaks no principle and excites no one has failed.**
- **When a strong idea causes a balance problem, keep the idea and fix the numbers.** Balance gets tuned in playtesting. An idea cut at the design stage is gone.
