# Unfolding

Principles for how unfolds work and how mechanics connect. Terms are defined in [taxonomy.md](taxonomy.md).

---

## A new mechanic is a game, not a power boost

A new mechanic starts fresh and its beginning progresses fast, but it's still a game with its own curve and its own promises.

- **It needs its own pacing.** If the player opens most of the mechanic and gets most of its useful improvements right away, they get a jump in power and then very little.
- **Its capacity opens slowly.** Space to work with should open gradually, and there should be more of it to open than seems necessary.
- **It needs depth.** Its own [expansions](taxonomy.md#expansion), and real [choices](choices.md).

---

## Connecting mechanics

### The point of a connection

A new mechanic's connection back to an existing one exists to **shorten the path to the next promise** in the existing mechanic.

Mechanic A became very slow: it was hard to make progress and the next promise was very far. The new mechanic shrinks that path dramatically.

**As long as this happens, the connection is doing its job.** Exactly how it happens is not that important.

### Too much is also a failure

A connection should shorten the path to the **next** promise, not carry the player past several. When a new mechanic's output lets the player blow through many promises in the old mechanic at once, those promises are cheapened and the old mechanic's curve breaks.

### The bonus: change the shape of an engine

The best connections **change the shape of an [engine](taxonomy.md#engine)**: something rare, linear, and flat suddenly becomes something you can produce and scale.

It's nice to be clever here: to have the progress feel fresh, or to shorten the time to the next promise in a way that is interesting or surprising. **That's a bonus, not a requirement.**
